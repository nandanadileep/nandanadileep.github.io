Some beliefs leave slowly

Recently, while reading Maxwell Maltz's Psycho-Cybernetics, I came across a passage from Bertrand Russell's The Conquest of Happiness.

Russell's point is simple: sometimes reason has already rejected a belief, but the rest of us has not. An old fear, guilt, or idea can still return automatically and feel true.

“Whenever it thrusts foolish thoughts or feelings into your consciousness, pull them up by the roots, examine them, and reject them.”

When that happens, look at the belief, ask where it came from, and remind yourself clearly what you actually believe now. Keep doing this until the newer belief reaches deeper than the old one.

I have been thinking about this lately. Knowing that something is irrational once is not always enough. Sometimes we have to reason with ourselves again and again, until we stop living by a belief we no longer hold.

Bertrand Russell, The Conquest of Happiness (1930), Chapter 7: “The Sense of Sin.”
